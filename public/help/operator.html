LiveStage Lights / Working prototype 0.1

Program. Preview. Calibrate.

One engine, a small operator screen, and an agent that can build the same cues you can edit.

Patch sheet

☰ → Patch lists every fixture like a Hog fixture window, grouped by type: ID (Hog user number), name, Hog universe and address range, Art-Net route, status and live level. The cards above show each Hog universe, its Art-Net route and a 512-channel map; the strip at the top shows the Mac → Hog network path and whether output is sending. Click a fixture to see the DMX bytes the engine is building for it right now; ✎ jumps to its Patch tab in Build. Bytes are only sent while output is armed, and Art-Net never confirms receipt.

Show and Build

The switch at the top changes the whole desk. Show is for running: pick a page on the left, its cue list is on the right, and GO (or Space) runs the next cue. Click a cue to stand it by, double-click to jump straight to it. ⌫ goes back. Build is for programming: edit cue lists, record looks, add effect presets, place fixtures, align beams and patch.

Pages and cue lists

Each page is a cue list. In Build, open a cue to set how it arrives — Cut or Fade with seconds — and what happens after: Wait for GO, or Auto-follow after a number of seconds. Add cues with Record look (captures every patched fixture as it looks now), Effect (one-click presets such as Strobe, Fade out, Breathe, Circle or Figure 8 on any group) or Library (reuse any saved cue; the same cue can sit on several pages). Non-looping cues go dark at the end unless Hold last frame is set in Edit cue content.

Preview a cue

The eye on any cue plays it in the visualizer only — the stage gets an amber frame and a PREVIEW banner while the real lights keep running the live cue. Flip the banner between Live and Preview to compare, press Send to live to GO it with its normal fade, or press Esc / × to stop previewing. The Preview buttons in the effect dialogs use the same preview, so they also work while output is armed.

Blind

Press Blind next to Show/Build to program without touching the real lights. Every Programmer and Override change goes to a blind layer shown only in the visualizer (violet frame and BLIND banner) — on top of the live look, or on top of a cue you are previewing. Record saves the blind look as a cue on the current page; Apply live fades the blind changes onto the real lights. Clear fades the blind layer away (two seconds by default; Settings → Blind, which also sets how long Apply live takes). Lamp strike/douse always acts on the real fixtures.

Visualizer or Groups

In Show and Build, the switch at the top right of the stage swaps the 3D visualizer for big group tiles. Each tile shows its fixtures as live color dots and the group's average level; tap to select (Shift to add), then Override or Clear. With Groups on, the visualizer stops completely — no 3D drawing and LED wall video paused — which keeps the show computer light. Output timing is unaffected either way: the engine runs separately.

Effects on a selection

Select fixtures, then open Effects (a Programmer tab in Build; Properties | Effects in Show's Override panel). Only effects that fit the selection appear — movement effects need moving heads. Effects layer on the current look: movement circles where the heads already point, intensity effects pulse the current level and color, color effects recolor. With Preview first, a tap shows the effect in the visualizer only (EFFECT banner); adjust speed, size, level and colors, then Apply live. With Straight to live, a tap goes to the real lights. Running effects are listed with a stop button; Clear also stops them.

Custom effects

Tune a previewed or running effect, then Save as custom effect to name it and pick an icon. Custom effects (marked with a small violet dot) live in the show next to the built-ins, appear for any matching selection, and can be previewed, applied and saved into cues like any other. Agents can design and save them too. Right-click a custom effect to delete it; built-ins cannot be changed.

Saving changes into cues

When anything is changed or an effect is running, Save in the GO bar lights up. Update merges your changes and effects into the playing cue — its own effects keep running, every page using it gets the update, and nothing visibly jumps. Save as new cue stores a copy of the playing cue plus your changes right after it and runs it. Under More, Replace freezes the whole current look. In Blind, Save records the blind changes.

Override and Clear

In Show, click fixtures or a group pill, then Override for quick intensity, color, position and beam controls. Overridden fixtures stay on top of every cue, even across GO. Clear (or C) fades all manual changes back to the running cue — three seconds by default. Change it, or choose Instant for a hard cut, in ☰ → Settings. The Clear button counts the overridden fixtures.

Groups, positions and colors

Right-click (or long-press) any group tile or pill to select it, add or remove the selected fixtures, set it to the selection, rename it, edit its fixtures or delete it. The New group tile (and the + pill in Build) opens the group editor: name it, start from the current selection, and tap fixtures — sorted by type — in the order chases should run. Group pills above the stage select fixtures; Movers is every patched moving head. In Build, the + pill saves the current selection as a group. Under Position, “Save for N” stores one named position for all selected heads; tapping it later recalls it for whichever of those heads are selected. White is always the first color.

LED wall

The LED wall button opens a tray of images and videos. Tap one to put it on the wall in the 3D preview; add your own with “Add image or video”. Fit and brightness are in the tray. This is preview-only — it does not drive the real screen — and videos pause while physical output is armed to keep the desk light.

Start with the demo

Open the app and select Prism dance → Play preview. The 60-second cue combines a backlight comet, color tides, alternating gold/blue pulses, mirrored blooms, spectrum movement and a white finale. Six Rogue heads answer with limited sweeps and prism changes. Agent · Aurora was created and played through the actual MCP adapter during verification.

Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, or use Front / Orbit / Top / Photo. Haze changes the drawn beams only. With haze at zero the emitters and floor pools remain, but the light shafts disappear.

Make your own look

  1. Select a group or individual fixture.
  2. Change color, intensity and supported beam properties. These are manual overrides of the current cue.
  3. Choose Save current look to capture all mapped fixtures into a reusable still cue.
  4. Clear overrides returns the selected fixtures to cue control. Starting another cue clears all overrides.

Color palettes are stored separately. A Rogue has a color wheel, so requested colors are mapped to the nearest solid wheel position; it cannot reproduce arbitrary RGB mixes like the COLORado washes.

Design and save effects

Design an effect offers a group, effect, colors, speed, intensity and duration. Preview it without saving, or save it into both the effect library and cue library. Agents can combine many tracks and named positions into a choreographed cue. Choose Look → Edit selected cue to tune a saved track. Full JSON is available only in the expandable advanced editor.

Each play takes a snapshot. Editing a saved cue changes its next playback, not the look currently running. One cue runs at a time; later active tracks override earlier tracks on overlapping fixtures. Smooth transitions between multiple independently running cues are future work.

Calibrate the moving heads

The photographed room establishes approximate geometry, not an exact mapping between pan/tilt and stage coordinates. Fixture IDs 301–306 still need physical left/right confirmation. The working rehearsal photo position was around coarse pan 125, tilt 144, but actual direction depends on mounting and fixture range settings.

  1. Start disarmed. Select a single Rogue, open Position, expand Calibration & limits.
  2. Inspect its current home and conservative limits. Only mark the review checkbox after checking those values on the real fixture using the Hog. Capture the position and record the fixture’s physical location.
  3. Set the safe limits and current home, then save. The six previously tested Rogues are enabled when physical output is armed; the review checkbox records spatial calibration only. For the first physical test, use one head at low intensity and verified bounds.
  4. Explicitly arm output, aim with the Position sliders, and use Capture current position to save a point such as Rooftop or Center stage. The same saved point is available through MCP.
  5. Disarm before editing bounds or patch. Use Patch to move the fixture model to the correct location. These coordinates only affect visualization.

An agent can use pointA and pointB in a track to interpolate between saved per-fixture positions. The preview does not solve inverse kinematics from a clicked stage point. Point names alone do not prove where the real beam lands.

Motor response

Position → Motor speed controls the Rogue’s physical pan/tilt speed channel. 100% sends DMX 0 (fastest); 0% sends DMX 255 (slowest). Fastest is the default. This is separate from the speed of a programmed effect or the joystick’s Fine/Normal/Fast sensitivity.

Joystick aiming

In Position, switch to Joystick. Hold and drag from the center: left/right changes pan and up/down changes tilt. The center is zero movement, not DMX position zero. Release, leave the window, or switch selection to stop; the head holds its new position. Arrow keys also work while the joystick has focus. Choose Fine, Normal, or Fast speed. A selected group moves relatively, preserving each head’s starting offsets within its limits. The pan/tilt sliders remain available below for exact values. Save the result as a named position.

Physical output and handback

Preview only → Arm physical output stops preview first and asks for an explicit live action. It does not start a cue. Choose Play live when ready. The master scales intensity; Blackout closes shutters and zeroes dimmers while retaining positions. Stop fades to black. Disarm sends several dark frames, then closes the sender.

Hog’s Art-Net input is a routing takeover, not an ordinary programmer layer. To return control, select the yielded DMX addresses in Hog and press Set to HogNet Input. Do not assume disconnecting the Mac restores the console. Use the illustrated setup guide for exact ranges.

Working with LiveStage

The lighting app is separate from the LED-wall app. A LiveStage OSC marker can trigger a named cue on localhost port 1461. The lighting engine runs the full timing sequence independently. UI, API, OSC and MCP all reach the same command handlers. The current transport is local-only; running lighting on another computer needs an explicitly configured network bridge in a later version.

A missed packet should be retried with the same execution ID, not a new one. The engine acknowledges accepted OSC commands; this is not a Hog reception acknowledgment. Video pause/seek reconciliation and shared timecode are not yet implemented.

Before a live show

  • Confirm exact personality, address and physical order of every fixture; unverified MH7/generic heads remain unpatched.
  • Rehearse the cue sequence, not only isolated effects. Review moving ranges and brightness in the actual room.
  • Export the show; keep a known working copy and a Hog fallback look.
  • Test cable loss, sender exit, engine restart, blackout and manual handback. This version always restarts disarmed.
  • Keep one active sender. Separate software does not protect against the whole Mac failing.

The native window can close without stopping the engine. Disarm before shutting the service down. A hard crash or network failure cannot guarantee the final dark packets arrive; the receiver may retain its last levels.

Masters

The masters button in the stage view tools swaps the fixture buttons under the stage for masters: square slots that each hold a cue. Click an empty one to choose a cue, or drag one from the Library onto it. Its key is written bold in the corner (A S D F G H J K L to start). Hold the key to flash the cue; let go and it stops. Clicking the slot does the same. The ⚙ on a slot (or right-click) changes what the key does: Flash, Toggle (press on, press off), Go (play from the top), Pause or Release, plus a fade, a label and the cue. To change a key, click it and press the new one; if another master or a desk shortcut already has it, the slot offers Swap or Take it. ← and → page through more masters; Add master adds one.

Scenes

The Scenes tab next to Pages keeps base looks. Save look as scene saves what the rig shows now; click a scene to make it active. The active scene stays on underneath every cue: fixtures no cue uses show it, and when you stop or release the cues the rig fades back to it instead of going dark. The × releases it. Right-click a scene to rename, recolour, update or delete it. The app never starts with a scene on, and output cannot be armed while a scene is active.

Library

Library, the third tab at the top, shows every saved cue as a tile where the stage usually is, with tags for its master keys, pages and triggers. Search and filter them, and drag a tile onto a master below. Click a tile to open its cue sheet on the right: how it plays, every page entry, master and trigger that fires it (with fade and follow), its OSC and MCP addresses, its effects and its saved look fixture by fixture. From the sheet you can rename, preview (the stage comes back; Back to the library returns), put it on a master, add it to the page, or delete it.

Tap tempo

Tap T (or the BPM button in the GO bar) on the beat. Cues written at a tempo follow it: their effects, chases and auto-follows speed up or slow down with the band. ½ and ×2 halve or double it; ↺ goes back to the cue's own tempo.

Master levels

The thin bar on the right of each master works like a fader: drag it to dim that master's cue. Raising it from zero starts the cue; back to zero releases it (if the fader started it).

Sound triggers

Turn on Sound triggers in Settings → Modules and choose the input device (Dante Virtual Soundcard). In a master's ⚙ pick the input channel the kick is on, then set Sensitivity against the live meter: each hit above the white line flashes the master. Hold is how long each hit lasts. During the show, Shift + the master's key (or its waveform badge, or the APC40 Activator buttons) switches a sound trigger on and off. For guitar, keys or pads choose Follow the level: the lights breathe with the instrument, brighter as it plays louder; Range sets how far above the line they reach full, Release how softly they fade.

MIDI and the APC40 mkII

Turn on MIDI in Settings → Modules. ProPresenter sees a MIDI input called StageLight: give a master a MIDI note out, or a cue a MIDI send, and map that note in ProPresenter. Plug in an APC40 mkII and it works straight away: pads are masters 1–40, faders are the levels of the fader bank (Bank ◀ ▶ moves it), scene buttons are scenes 1–5 in their own colours, Play is GO, Tap tempo taps. Edit APC40 profile… shows the unit on screen: press a control to find it, click it to change what it does.

Before the show

☰ → Pre-show check lists the Art-Net path, routing, fixtures, last saved version and modules, runs the rig check, and locks the show. The padlock in the top bar locks editing (cues, pages, patch…) for the performance while playback keeps working; hold it for a second to unlock. Settings → Show versions restores an earlier version of the show. If the engine ever restarts during a show, a banner offers to Resume where you were: output, scene and cue.

Panels

Drag the inner edge of the left or right panel to resize it; double-click the edge to reset it. The button on the edge hides or shows the panel.

Connection & diagnostics shows local IPv4 interfaces, last send error, packet count, and intervals where the engine was delayed. “Sending” means the OS accepted UDP output; check Hog’s red DMX values to verify reception. Audit events are saved to data/audit.jsonl.

What this first version does not claim

No calibrated photometry, true gobo image projection, complete room measurements, automated fixture identification, hardware emergency stop, automatic backup sender, timecode synchronization or unattended production certification. The three-dimensional model is a useful spatial sketch. The installed Node runtime is required by the Mac launcher; it is not bundled.
